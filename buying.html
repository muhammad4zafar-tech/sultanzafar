Buying a Home

STEP 1: LOCATION, TYPES, NEW HOME or RESALE

Living in an urban setting offers convenience and amenities, while suburban living often provides more space and affordability.

Choosing a Neighborhood

Consider future development, zoning, and property value trends. Contact your municipal office for details.

Questions to Ask

  • Are new developments planned nearby?
  • Any zoning changes?
  • New highways or subdivisions?
  • Restrictions on home‑based businesses?

Types of Homes

  • Single Detached
  • Semi‑Detached
  • Duplex
  • Townhouse
  • Link / Carriage
  • High‑Rise Condominium

New Homes

Advantages

  • Modern standards
  • Builder warranties
  • Lower maintenance

Disadvantages

  • Incomplete amenities
  • Construction noise
  • Young landscaping

Re‑Sale Homes

Advantages

  • Established neighborhoods
  • Mature landscaping
  • Upgrades may exist

Disadvantages

  • Higher maintenance
  • Possible renovations
  • Professional inspection recommended

STEP 2: Costs to Be Aware Of

Appraisal fees, taxes, surveys, insurance, land transfer tax, legal fees, inspections, moving costs, and more.

STEP 3: Mortgage Basics

Reduce interest by increasing down payment, shortening amortization, or making extra payments.

Pre‑Approved Mortgage

Provides certainty and locks in your rate for 60–90 days.

STEP 4: The Offer

Includes legal names, price, chattels, financing, possession date, survey, expiry, and conditions.

STEP 5: Closing Day

Your lawyer receives funds, pays the seller, registers the home, and gives you the keys.
